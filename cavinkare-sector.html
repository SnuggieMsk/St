B · Company history — the 42-year arc from a Rs 15,000 sachet-shampoo startup to a Rs 1,940 Cr Chennai FMCG conglomerate

B.1 The pre-history — Chinni Krishnan and the sachet idea (1970s-1983)

The CavinKare story begins with Chinni Krishnan, C.K. Ranganathan's father — a Cuddalore pharmacist-entrepreneur who founded Beauty Cosmetics (maker of "Velvette" shampoo) and is widely credited as the original conceiver of the shampoo-sachet idea — making premium personal-care affordable in single-use Rs 1 portions. He died in 1982; the FMCG legacy was divided among his sons.

B.2 Founding (1983) — C.K. Ranganathan starts Chik India with Rs 15,000

The youngest son, C.K. Ranganathan, started his own venture in 1983 in Cuddalore with ~Rs 15,000 startup capital. He launched Chik shampoo — named in honour of his father Chinni Krishnan — in Rs 1 sachets targeted at rural + daily-wage consumers ignored by multinationals. Current legal entity (then Chik India Pvt Ltd) incorporated 22 March 1990.

B.3 The sachet revolution (1983-1992) — Chik becomes #1 in South India

C.K. Ranganathan's genius was distribution + pricing innovation. Innovations included the empty-sachet buy-back scheme, aggressive village-level distribution, and Rs 1-2 pricing discipline. By 1992 Chik became the #1 shampoo brand in South India — ahead of multinational incumbents. The sachet revolution became a Harvard/IIM case study on bottom-of-pyramid market creation.

B.4 The CavinKare rebrand (1998) — from single-product to FMCG platform

In 1998 an internal employee contest produced the name CavinKare — combining "Cavin" (Tamil for beauty/grace) with the initials of Chinni Krishnan. The rebrand marked the shift from single-product to a multi-brand FMCG platform: Meera (herbal hair-care), Nyle, Spinz, Indica + Karthika (hair-colour), Fairever, Raaga.

B.5 Diversification (2000-2010) — salons, fruit-pulp, dairy

CavinKare expanded beyond personal care: Trends In Vogue Pvt Ltd established 2000 — pioneering the "Family Salon" concept in India (Green Trends + Limelite + Trends In Vogue & Academy). Maa Fruits Pvt Ltd acquired Feb 2008 for ~Rs 27.6 Cr (mango/guava/pineapple/apple juices + fruit-pulp). In 2009 CavinKare bought a Kanchipuram dairy farm to launch Cavin's dairy.

B.6 The 2010-2024 build — FMCG full-stack + dairy scale + second-generation transition

Through 2010s-2020s CavinKare built a true 5-vertical FMCG platform. Dairy scaled aggressively; CavinKare announced near-doubling of Kanchipuram + Erode capacity 3.8 → 6 lakh litres/day with value-added focus (protein-rich cheese). Second generation — Manuranjith + Dharaneeswari — joined the Board. C.K. Ranganathan built Ka Enterprises, an angel-investment vehicle backing 50+ TN startups. FY25 revenue Rs 1,940 Cr; PAT +587.61%.

B.7 Why this matters for the IBank pitch

(1) C.K. Ranganathan is a genuine first-generation FMCG builder — the sachet revolution is a cited market-creation case study. (2) 100% closely-held Ranganathan family — sizeable, multi-generational, uninstitutionalised family-PB. (3) 5-vertical structure means a single bank can win 5+ distinct product flows. (4) Cavin's dairy capex (3.8 → 6 lakh litres/day) is a committed Rs 150-300 Cr financing window. (5) ~1,300 distributors + 25 lakh+ retail outlets = one of TN's largest unbanked distributor-SCF opportunities. (6) Ka Enterprises startup-ecosystem creates a family-office + PB adjacency rare among traditional FMCG promoters.

D · Value-chain — 5-vertical FMCG cluster — personal care + dairy + snacks + beverages + salons

CavinKare operates five distinct FMCG value-chains under one promoter group. (1) Personal Care: palm-derivatives + surfactants + herbal extracts + packaging film → blending/filling/sachet-packing → ~1,300 distributors → 25 lakh+ retail outlets (kirana + modern-trade + e-commerce). (2) Cavin's Dairy: raw-milk procurement (Kanchipuram + Erode catchment farmers) → chilling + processing + UHT/value-added → cold-chain distribution → retail + institutional. (3) Garden Snacks: edible-oil + flour + spices → namkeen manufacturing → distributor network. (4) Maa Beverages: seasonal fruit procurement (mango/guava, Apr-Jul peak) → pulping + juice/tetra-pack → retail + B2B. (5) Trends Salons: Green Trends + Limelite + Trends In Vogue franchise outlets → styling + beauty services + product retail.

H · Question bank

12 questions for the meeting

  1. Cavin's dairy capacity expansion — what is the capex split (Kanchipuram vs Erode; processing vs cold-chain)?
  2. What is the value-added dairy revenue mix today and FY28 target (cheese, protein, premium)?
  3. FMCG distributor network — how many distributors, what is the average credit period, and which bank does channel-finance today?
  4. Personal-care portfolio — which of the 13 brands are growing vs which are being rationalised?
  5. Maa Fruits — is it B2B pulp-supply-led or consumer-juice-led; what is the seasonal WC peak?
  6. Trends In Vogue — Green Trends + Limelite outlet count and franchise-expansion plans?
  7. Palm-oil + packaging input — hedge ratio and procurement strategy?
  8. Second-generation transition — how are Manuranjith and Dharaneeswari's mandates structured?
  9. Ka Enterprises — is the startup-investment activity inside CavinKare or a separate family-office vehicle?
  10. Is there any IPO / PE / strategic-stake plan on a 3-5 year horizon?
  11. What is the open MCA charges ~Rs 250 Cr split by bank across the group entities?
  12. Family-PB — is the Ranganathan family wealth held via HUF / family-trust or individually?

What NOT to ask

  • Do not ask about the broader Chinni Krishnan family FMCG entities — CavinKare is C.K. Ranganathan's independent build; the topic is sensitive.
  • Do not pitch on rate — FMCG groups run tight cost-discipline.
  • Do not propose family-succession or ESOP advisory in the first meeting.
  • Do not name specific competitor banks unless asked.
  • Do not over-pitch an IPO — raise it only as optionality, the family decides timing.
I · Objection handling

"We are happy with our current FMCG WC banks (HDFC + SBI + Axis + Kotak)."

"Mr. Ranganathan — we are not displacing the WC consortium. We want the Cavin's dairy capex sleeve (which is incremental and partly greenfield), the distributor-SCF layer (which the consortium has not built), and the family-PB mandate. Three additive sleeves, not a displacement."

"Your rates are not competitive vs PSU / large private."

"FMCG WC rates are commoditised. Our differentiation: a sustainability-linked covenant on dairy cold-chain energy-efficiency; distributor-SCF at scale; family-office + PB platform; and IPO/DCM readiness when that conversation comes. We compete on structure and partnership, not headline rate."

"Distributor finance — our distributors arrange their own working capital."

"Exactly the inefficiency. An anchor-led SCF programme on CavinKare's credit gives your distributors cheaper funding, improves your collection cycle, and deepens channel loyalty. It is a win for the distributor, for CavinKare's WC, and a clean annuity for us."

"CavinKare is a private family company — we keep banking relationships simple."

"That is precisely why a single relationship bank across all five verticals + the family-PB makes sense — simplicity through consolidation, not fragmentation. One bank that understands personal care + dairy + snacks + beverages + salons + the family."

"Ranganathan family wealth is private."

"That is the design point of our PB platform — we are the most discrete on senior promoter PB. Our partner can fly down to discuss multi-generation structuring + the Ka Enterprises startup-portfolio. No commitment."

"You don't understand mass-market FMCG / the sachet economy."

"We bank Marico, Dabur, Emami, Hatsun Agro and several regional FMCG leaders. We can bring the FMCG-cluster team — distributor-SCF specialist, dairy-capex underwriter, consumer-sector analyst — for a working session."
