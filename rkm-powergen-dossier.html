The June MPC rate-lock window — why this matters to every Tier-1 relationship

The next MPC is 4–6 June 2026. Goldman's pricing is 50 bp hike; the street consensus is 25 bp. Either way, any Tier-1 working capital renewal, term loan sanction, or NCD issuance that closes before the MPC books the current 5.25% repo — the MCLR / T-bill linkage saves 25–50 bp over a typical 3–5 year tenor. Every dossier below carries a '30-day rate-lock' action in Section 14 for this exact reason.

What moves with oil at $96–100

  • Specialty chemicals, agrochem, polymers: input cost pass-through lag → 150–250 bp gross margin compression in FY27 Q1; short-term working-capital gap opens.
  • Refiners (CPCL, MRPL, IOC): GRM upside Rs 2–3/bbl for the two-month window; inventory gain on crude-in-transit; trade-finance volumes spike.
  • Textile, FMCG, retail: freight-inland diesel pass-through 80–110 bp in Q1; demand-side softening if pumps repass fuel >3%.

What moves if monsoon is 92% LPA

  • Sugar, dairy, edible oils: cane tonnage risk in Maharashtra / Karnataka; SMP / milk procurement cost ↑ 4–7%.
  • Textiles: cotton MSP revision likely; raw cotton cost ↑ 8–12% into the ginning season.
  • Rural FMCG, 2W, tractors: kharif demand de-rates 150–300 bp for H2 FY27.
  • Thermal IPPs: coal shortage tail risk if hydro under-delivers; merchant tariffs firm Rs 0.50–1.20/kWh above LTA.

US–India trade deal: the 31 July / 29 September cliff

The July framework compressed US reciprocal tariffs from 50% → 18% on most lines; generic pharma exports at 0%, patented at 100%.[6] Electronics / smartphones / auto-comp land at 15–18%. Two working deadlines: 31 Jul 2026 (generic pharma zero-duty shipments clear US ports) and 29 Sep 2026 (patented molecule 100% tariff goes live, forcing a onshore-manufacture vs re-export decision for branded players). Working-capital cycle tightens by 30–45 days for exporters who need to front-load Q2 shipments.

Rupee regime

USD/INR tested 94.63 before RBI sold ~$12 bn in April FX market operations.[3] CAD widening to 2.0% of GDP[5] and FII equity outflows of ~$4.1 bn MTD (April)[7] are the weight; expect 92–95 range through Q2 FY27 with RBI smoothing. What this tells us — three points

  1. PFC is the incumbent and the structural counterparty. Rs 22,313 Cr of face-value charges across 5 tranches, most dating to the 2017 consolidation with a 2020 modification. PFC is the DFI mandated to support power projects and prices floating-rate at ~PLR-linked (effective ~9.75%). This is the displaceable lender — but only with regulatory finesse because PFC has loan covenants and cross-default clauses that need careful handling.
  2. NCD position (Rs 8,597 Cr face) is held in trust for public bondholders. Not displaceable without calling the NCD or arranging a bond tender. Typically held to maturity; refinancing requires market-timing discipline.
  3. Indian Bank (Rs 355 Cr, 2015) is a legacy position. Small ticket; never modified since creation. Likely a long-forgotten WC consortium seat that has been left in place. IBank can approach Indian Bank for a secondary swap or a takeover of that slot without disturbing the PFC anchor.

05.8 — Decarbonisation trajectory for thermal IPPs

India’s net-zero target (2070) allows thermal plants to operate through their economic life. However, structural margin compression is expected post-2035 as:

  • Solar tariffs have fallen to Rs 2.40–2.80/kWh; BESS at Rs 3.80–4.50/kWh for 4-hour storage
  • FGD retrofit adds 30–45 paise/kWh to variable cost
  • Coal pricing formula shifts toward imported-parity by FY30
  • I-REC / carbon market pricing matures; negative-cost for thermal-linked offsets possible
  • Long-term PPA tenors at state-level may shorten from 25 to 10–15 years post-FY30

For R.K.M, this implies: (i) any new TL should be tenor-capped at 10 years; (ii) ESG covenants on FGD + eventual green-adjacency investments; (iii) NCD refinancing tenor should match PPA remaining life, not plant physical life.

05.9 — FGD retrofit — the compliance capex

CPCB mandate for 1,440 MW supercritical plants: commission Flue-Gas Desulphurisation by December 2026[21]. Capex guidance: Rs 40–50 lakh per MW = Rs 580–720 Cr for R.K.M’s full capacity. Partial pass-through to DISCOMs through a tariff-reopener clause is possible (as with NTPC plants) but not guaranteed. Funding gap: Rs 300–450 Cr is the likely IBank term-loan opportunity if the refinance engagement is consummated.

12.3 — Litigation & regulatory file — Fatehpur East coal-block matter

This is the single most material item in the entity’s history. Briefly: the 2008 allotment of the Fatehpur East coal block was struck down by the Supreme Court in 2014 (along with all coal blocks allocated 1993–2010). CBI later investigated the allotment as part of the “Coal Scam” cases. ED initiated PMLA proceedings, attaching Rs 1,900+ Cr of assets and freezing Rs 912 Cr in FDs / mutual funds across 2024[65]. The matter has now (Nov 2025) been materially resolved in R.K.M’s favour through a sequence of judicial actions:

✓ Madras HC, July 2025 — ED writ petitions allowed

In W.P. Nos. 4297 & 4300 of 2025, the Madras High Court held that “when there is no predicate offence, initiation of proceedings under PMLA is a non-starter” and reiterated that ED is “not a super cop to investigate anything and everything which comes to its notice”[66]. The court emphasised that filing of a positive final report by the CBI does not automatically give the ED jurisdiction to inquire into matters not covered by the chargesheet. Substantive impact: undermines the basis of the ED attachment proceedings.

✓ Special CBI Court, November 2025 — full discharge

Special court for coal-block-allocation cases discharged R.K.M Powergen Pvt Ltd, former Coal Secretary H.C. Gupta, and three other accused in the Fatehpur East coal block allocation case, holding there was no evidence to prosecute[67]. Mudajaya Group’s India unit publicly confirmed the discharge to KL exchange[68]. Substantive impact: predicate criminal offence falls; ED PMLA action is correspondingly weakened.

✓ Operating record across the matter

Throughout the 2014–2025 litigation arc, the plant operated continuously on Coal India linkage (no production from the revoked Fatehpur block was ever undertaken) [64]. PPA receivables continued to accrue, debt service continued on schedule, and the entity executed the FY21→FY25 turnaround (PAT −Rs 180 Cr to +Rs 547 Cr). Operating performance was decoupled from the legal overhang.

(i) Status of ED appeal to Supreme Court (if any); (ii) status of attached assets (release schedule); (iii) NCLT writ-petition history (RKM had previously moved Madras HC in 2018 to restrain banks from approaching NCLT)[69]; (iv) Mudajaya parent KL legal exposures relevant to ring-fencing; (v) coal-block-revocation-related compensation claims (if any) under Coal Mines (Special Provisions) Act 2015.
