5 · Structural findings & manager/distributor behaviour

5.1 The five structural defects (facts, not opinions)

  1. 100% Regular plans. Every one of the 27 folios is a Regular plan. Weighted regular-vs-direct TER gap ≈ 1.0pp/yr on equity funds (e.g. UTI SC 1.14pp, ICICI Innovation 0.93pp, ABSL MAA 0.99pp — AMC factsheets May-Jun-2026). On the ₹4.62 cr MF book that is ≈ ₹4.8 lakh/yr of embedded commission (calc). Compounded 10 years at 12% gross, the drag costs ≈ ₹1.28 cr of terminal wealth (calc, assumptions in §16).
  2. Distributor conflict of interest. Axis Bank distributes the portfolio; Axis AMC (bank affiliate) supplies 9 of 20 schemes (22.1% of investments incl. its multi-asset); Axis Max Life (bank associate, 19.99% Axis-owned) supplies all 4 insurance policies; both PMS were sold through the same channel. Nothing here is illegal — but product selection correlates suspiciously well with what the bank earns trail/commission on, and includes Axis funds rated 1★ (Multi Asset Allocation) and brand-new NFOs with no track record (Momentum, Value 50, Manufacturing, Business Cycles).
  3. NFO-chasing. At least 8 purchases were made at or near ₹10–12 NAV in NFOs/new funds (Kotak Multicap ₹10.00, ICICI Flexicap ₹10.00, Axis Momentum ₹10.00, Axis India Mfg ₹10.00/₹12.48, Axis Business Cycles ₹10.28, ICICI Innovation ₹11.17, Axis Nifty500 V50 ₹10.20, UTI SC via 2020 NFO era pricing — statement avg-cost column). NFOs pay the highest first-year distributor commissions. Some worked (Kotak Multicap +94%), the newest have not (Momentum −13%).
  4. Over-diversification without diversification. 20 schemes + 2 PMS across 8 AMCs, yet §14 shows the equity sleeve is one big bet on the same ~60 large/mid-cap names (HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, L&T, Maruti/TVS, Reliance, Infosys appear in nearly every equity fund). Six positions are <₹5 L (2.9% of investments combined) — too small to matter, big enough to complicate taxes and monitoring. Four separate asset-allocation products (ABSL MAA, ICICI MA, Axis MAA, Axis BAF) do the same job.
  5. Buying after the re-rating. The three ICICI India Opportunities folios tell the story: first tranche at ₹22.72 (+56%), then ₹36.87 and ₹37.82 bought later — both now underwater vs NAV ₹35.54. Same with Axis Multicap (₹18.13/₹18.00 tranches flat-to-negative) and both PMS (entered ~2024-25 highs; Helios −7.9%, Founders −4.0%). The pattern: winners are topped up after they have run, at materially worse prices — a distributor-cycle signature, not a valuation-driven process.

5.2 What the portfolio is economically betting on (Stage 2 aggregate)

Aggregating disclosed fund portfolios (31-May-2026 factsheets) weighted by position size: the book is long Indian financials (~18-20% look-through), autos & auto components (~8-10%), capital goods / manufacturing capex (~9-11%), domestic consumption, with ~8.6% precious metals and ~15% debt/cash. It is overwhelmingly a domestic-cyclical, large+midcap growth book with a manufacturing/capex tilt from the thematic funds and mid/small-cap risk concentrated inside MO Founders PMS (73% mid+small), Kotak funds and the two small-cap funds. There is no meaningful export/IT overweight, no international equity, and no standalone duration. In a domestic-demand slowdown or a capex-cycle stall, most of the book falls together — the diversification is largely cosmetic (see §14 overlap matrix).
