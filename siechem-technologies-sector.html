B · Company history — the 32-year arc from family trader to Tier-1 specialty-cable OEM

B.1 Founding (1994-2001) — the trading + assembly years

Siechem Technologies Private Limited was incorporated 04 May 1994 in Chennai (CIN U67100TN1994PTC027463)[952] by founder Damodaran Pondy and co-promoter Padma Damodaran (both directors since incorporation). The first 7-8 years were spent in cable trading + small-batch electrical-cable assembly out of Chennai, with the Damodaran family acting as distributor for imported European specialty cable into Indian industrial accounts. The classification under MCA "U67100" (other financial activities) reflects the trading-era SIC; the actual operations have since pivoted to manufacturing.

B.2 Pivot to manufacturing (2002) — the Pondicherry plant

Commercial-scale specialty-cable manufacturing began in 2002 from the Sedarapet (Pondicherry) plant[951]. The initial plant footprint was modest — a single extrusion line running PVC + XLPE compound with bought-in copper conductor. Customer base in this phase was domestic industrial / power / telecom. The decision to set up in Pondicherry rather than Tamil Nadu proper reflected the GST-era tax-incentive arbitrage at the time.

B.3 Up the value chain (2004-2010) — proprietary R&D + electron-beam crosslinking

From the mid-2000s, Siechem invested progressively in proprietary R&D, electron-beam (e-beam) crosslinking, and in-house compounding. The shift from PVC commodity cable to XLPE/electron-beam-crosslinked specialty cable opened access to higher-margin segments — aerospace (DO-160), automotive (ISO/TS-16949 then IATF-16949), medical (IEC-60601), and industrial-automation (UL/CSA grades). The plant footprint grew from ~25,000 sq ft initial to 100,000 sq ft built-up area by FY18-19, with multi-stage SPM (special-purpose machine) lines, in-house metallurgical lab, R&D centre, and electron-beam unit[951]. Director Arunkumar Muthukumar (Gummidipundi) joined the board on 25 Apr 2008[952] bringing operations + R&D leadership.

B.4 Multi-segment scale-out (2011-2020) — 34 markets + 23 Mn part numbers

Through the 2010s Siechem expanded from a single specialty grade into 34 distinct end-markets with over 23 Mn part numbers[951] — aerospace (DO-160 qualified for commercial aviation tier-2 supply to Boeing/Airbus India sourcing), automotive harness (ISO/TS-16949 → IATF-16949), high-voltage EV (600V/1000V), renewable cable (TUV-certified solar DC + wind AC), telecom (LSZH low-smoke zero-halogen), medical (IEC-60601), defence-grade (MIL-spec), industrial-automation (UL/CSA), data-centre, instrumentation, fire-survival cable (BS6387 / IEC 60331), and more. Director Patanjali joined the board on 08 Jan 2016[952] as technology + new-segment + Bhiwadi-expansion lead.

B.5 Inflection (2021-2024) — revenue doubles + Bhiwadi announced

FY23 revenue Rs ~570 Cr (analyst-estimated trailing-equivalent). FY24 revenue Rs 866 Cr (+52%)[953]; EBITDA growth +21.4%; book networth growth +16.8%. The 1-yr revenue CAGR is 15% but the explosive FY23-24 step suggests order-book conversion + capacity-utilisation maxing out the Pondicherry plant. The Bhiwadi (Delhi-NCR) Phase-2 expansion was announced in 2024 — 300,000 sq ft greenfield, scheduled commissioning December 2026[954]. Capex envelope (analyst-est) Rs 250-450 Cr split among civil-works, machinery, e-beam unit, R&D centre, and working-capital ramp. The Bhiwadi location places Siechem within proximity of the Delhi-NCR auto-OEM cluster (Maruti Suzuki, Honda, Hero, Yamaha) and the Northern aerospace/defence ecosystem (HAL Korwa, BEL Ghaziabad, defence PSU adjacencies).

B.6 The next 36 months (FY26-FY29) — capacity step + segment deepening

Bhiwadi commissioning Dec 2026 takes Siechem from ~10,000 km/day (Pondicherry only) to 5 million metres/day across the two-plant network — approximately 15 Mn conductor-km/yr[951]. Revenue trajectory base case: FY25 Rs ~1,000 Cr → FY26 Rs ~1,180 Cr → FY27 Rs ~1,450 Cr → FY28 Rs ~1,750 Cr (CAGR ~22% over the capex window). EBITDA margin expansion expected from 14% FY25 to ~16-17% FY28 as the EV-auto-harness + aerospace + renewable mix grows (these are 18-22% gross-margin segments vs 12-14% in commodity cable).

B.7 Why this matters for the IBank pitch

The 32-year arc tells you three things: (1) Damodaran is a builder, not a trader — he reinvested every cycle of margin into capability (e-beam, compounding, certifications) rather than dividending out; (2) the company is at a genuine inflection point — not a "we want capex" pitch but "we have already announced Bhiwadi and we need it funded"; (3) the family is still 92.83% concentrated — the Damodaran family-PB opportunity is real, multi-generational, and has not been institutionalised on any bank's PB platform.

D · Sector value-chain — specialty cable cluster + where Siechem sits

Specialty cable is a raw-material-intensive, certification-gated sub-segment of the broader Indian wires & cables industry (FY25 ~Rs 80-90k Cr). The specialty/aerospace/auto-EV/renewable-grade tier is ~Rs 22-28k Cr (CAGR 12-16% vs commodity-cable 7-9%). Siechem is a Tier-1 domestic specialty player (4-5 firms in this tier nationally) competing primarily with Cords Cable, Dynamic Cables, Ducab India, plus the LS Cable / Sumitomo / Prysmian foreign-MNC subsidiaries who dominate the aerospace + EV-premium top-end.

I · Objection handling — comebacks for the predictable pushback

"We are happy with our current banks."

"Mr. Damodaran — we are not asking to displace anyone. The Rs 473.66 Cr open charges are spread across a multi-bank consortium. Bhiwadi is going to add another Rs 250-450 Cr of capex requirement in the next 18 months. We are asking to be in that consortium — on capex, on FX, on LME hedge, and on family-PB. Not displacing, adding."

"Your rates are not competitive vs PSU."

"Capex TL rates are within 10-15 bps across all top-tier banks. Our differentiation is structural: a sustainability-linked covenant that gives you a 5 bps step-down when the EV+renewable mix crosses 40%; an LME-copper IRS that PSU consortium cannot price; a 7-day term-sheet vs PSU 21-28 day cycle; and a family-PB platform PSU consortium does not have. We compete on structure, not headline rate."

"You are too small to lead our Bhiwadi capex consortium."

"We do not need to lead. We need a Rs 100-150 Cr co-anchor seat on Bhiwadi. Once we deliver Phase-1 commissioning on time, the FY28 refresh becomes a different conversation. Show us one Rs 100 Cr sleeve where we can prove capex execution speed."

"You don't understand specialty cable / aerospace / EV harness."

"Our wholesale-banking team has worked with KEI Industries, RR Kabel, Polycab, Cords Cable, and three of the global Tier-1 wire-harness suppliers. We can bring the cable-cluster lead, capex underwriter, FX dealer, and aerospace export-finance specialist to the Bhiwadi technical due-diligence visit if useful."

"Our family wealth is private and we do not discuss it."

"That is exactly the point of our PB platform — we are the most discrete on India's senior promoter PB. Our partner can fly down to discuss governance + multi-generation structuring + ESOP for the senior R&D team. No commitment, no proposal — just a 30-minute conversation about how families like yours have institutionalised wealth without losing control."

"Your turnaround time on credit decisions is slow."

"Our credit committee meets weekly with a 7-day SLA on standard ticket sizes. PSU peers run 21-28 day cycles. We will write that SLA into our term-sheet for Bhiwadi capex."
